The ninth-largest country on Earth

Endless steppe, snow peaks and two modern capitals. Kazakhstan covers 2.7 million km² between Europe and Asia.

Places to visit

Astana

The capital, known for futuristic architecture on the Ishim River.

Almaty

The largest city, at the foot of the Trans-Ili Alatau mountains.

Charyn Canyon

A red-rock gorge about 200 km from Almaty, often compared to the Grand Canyon.

Lake Kaindy

A mountain lake with spruce trunks standing in turquoise water.

Turkistan

Home of the 14th-century Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi.

Baikonur

The world's first and largest space launch site.

Quick facts

Population

About 20 million people, living in one of the least densely populated countries in the world.

Languages

Kazakh is the state language. Russian is widely used in daily life and business.

Currency

The tenge (KZT), introduced in 1993.

Climate

Strongly continental: hot summers, cold winters, and big swings between seasons.

Nature

Steppe, deserts, forests and the Altai and Tien Shan mountains in one country.

Independence

Declared on 16 December 1991. Celebrated as Independence Day.

Nomad traditions

For centuries Kazakhs lived as nomadic herders. That heritage lives on in the yurt, the dombra (a two-stringed instrument), and kokpar and other horse games.

Guests are welcomed with tea and beshbarmak, the national dish of boiled meat and noodles.

Travel questions

May to September for cities and lakes. December to March for skiing near Almaty.

Many nationalities can enter visa-free for a limited stay. Check the official foreign ministry site before you travel.

Domestic flights and the Talgo and Tulpar trains connect the main cities. Distances are large, so plan time.
